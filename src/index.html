What this is

EC City Tour is an isometric city where every building is one Smarsh Enterprise Conduct microservice. A van representing a single regulated communication — a chat message, an email, a voice transcript — leaves the Customs House (ec-archive-gateway) carrying nothing but an S3 pointer and a gcid. It downloads the document from S3, qualifies the participants against surveillance pipelines, runs policy evaluation including an optional Cognition call, checks the quota sampler, and then forks: sampled communications go up to the Assembly Hall (ec-alerting-service), the Fingerprint File (ec-echo-engine), and the Rail Yard (ec-indexer); all communications end at the Records Hall (ec-centralised-audit-reporting). At every stop the van picks up one verdict and files one audit receipt.

The gauge on the van's flank is the accumulated end-to-end latency in milliseconds. The crate in the bed shows how much of the original document remains after minification. Both read straight from the model.

Fidelity ledger

Genuinely computed, live, in your browser: S3 chunk sizing and wave count (a faithful port of FileChunkingStrategy.maxAllowedChunkSizeBytes), Kafka queue depth, KEDA replica scaling via clamp(ceil(lag / lagThreshold), minReplicas, maxReplicas), retry-ladder timing (retry-0 and retry-1 delays per service, measured from source), quota counter using the Redis INCR model, pipeline verdict fan-out, ES bulk flush cost for the indexer, echo duplicate fingerprinting, and end-to-end latency accumulation across all 9 stations.

Assumed (documented in js/model.js): S3 first-byte latency 25 ms, S3 throughput 60 KB/ms, minification ratio 0.12 (12% of raw body survives), MongoDB read 4 ms, MongoDB write 8 ms, MongoDB bulk upsert 15 ms, Redis atomic increment 1 ms, Elasticsearch bulk base cost 40 ms + 200 KB/ms indexing throughput, policy evaluation 0.5 ms per policy, Cognition RTT 45,000 ms, enrichment fan-out (slowest of four parallel HTTP fetches) 60 ms, 6 participants per document, max-poll-records 50 where not overridden by service config.

Scaled down for screen time: The Cognition wait ceiling is 9,000,000 ms (the measured COMS_TIMEOUT in ec-surveillance-policy-evaluator). At true scale the van would be stationary for 2.5 hours. The quota sampling window and echo TTL (14 days, measured) are represented symbolically.

Faked (scenery only): Building shapes, road surface colours, vehicle livery, and all decorative landmark details. Treat the town as an illustration and the panel numbers as the lesson.

The branch in the road

At the Weighbridge (ec-quota-manager) the van reaches a fork. If the communication falls within the sampling quota — determined by a Redis atomic INCR and a hash of the gcid — it takes the left road to the alerting and indexing district. If not, it takes the short audit road directly to the Records Hall. Drag the Sampling % slider down toward 1% and watch how rarely the left road gets used.

Pacing

The first time the van reaches a station it stops for long enough to read that station's write-up (between 9 and 26 seconds, proportional to word count). A progress bar under the panel text shows how much time remains. Once every station has been explained, later trips run at speed with no stops. Space holds any stop indefinitely; S advances one station; R (⟲) replays the slow tour from the beginning; Run starts a fresh trip without resetting what you have read.

Three trips run in a row. On the second and third the van is faster because it skips read stops — but the latency numbers on its flank stay the same. That gap is the point: the service cost is determined by Kafka lag and replica count, not by how often you have read the write-up.

Controls

  • Space play / pause · S next station · R replay tour · F follow · L labels
  • Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, click any building for its write-up.
  • The ⤢ button, or a double-click, fits the whole city in view.
  • Drag Ingest Rate up to find the bottleneck — the first service whose bar turns red.
